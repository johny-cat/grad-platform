### https://cbs.umn.edu/bmbb/graduate-program/prospective-students/bmbb-graduate-program-faqs

> [source line 36] December 1: Application deadline

> [source line 87] Research Experience! The most successful applicants have significant and productive research experience(s). Evidence of intellectual independence in their research is favorably viewed upon. For example, they are able to effectively describe the rationale and biological context for their research, as well as what was accomplished. Exceptional research experience (Master’s Degrees, many years of experience, publications, etc.) may offset minor deficiencies in GPA.

> [source line 93] Competitive applications will have a GPA of 3.3 or better (on a 4.0 scale). If your GPA falls below 3.3, additional graduate level courses or degrees can be taken to demonstrate academic ability.

> [source line 112] Students join our program from a variety of backgrounds. Recommended academic preparation includes one year each of calculus, organic chemistry, and basic biology, including biochemistry and genetics. For students of demonstrated ability, background deficiencies can be made up during the first year of graduate study. Successful applicants must have previous research experience in an academic or industrial setting in addition to any course-related laboratory experiences.

> [source line 134] The program pays students a graduate fellowship or stipend and also covers tuition, student fees, and health insurance. The minimum required hourly rate is $27.00, but in BMBB the minimum rate of pay in the Fall of 2026 will be $36.77/hour. Specific terms and conditions of that offer will come in a separate letter. Funding for graduate students in BMBB typically comes from a combination of sources, including research assistant positions supported by the BMBB Graduate program and your Ph.D.

> [source line 141] If you opt-in to the University’s health care benefits, 5% of the premium costs (roughly $245 per semester in the fall and spring) is paid by the graduate students per semester towards their medical benefits. The department or the advisor pay the bulk of the cost for medical coverage.

> [source line 176] Applicants apply to the BMBB graduate program and not to a position in a specific lab. There is no upfront guarantee that a student will join a particular lab for their graduate studies. It is therefore important for students, prior to applying to our program, to identify multiple faculty whose research they find interesting and for whom they would want to work.

> [source line 177] At the beginning of the program in August, students go to the Itasca Biological Field Station for a week-long course. During that time, they have the opportunity to meet with faculty during modules and talks. After that week, there is an additional week during which students are encouraged to contact faculty about rotating in their labs.

> [source line 178] Once the school year starts, students participate in three 7-week rotations in faculty research laboratories with the first beginning in September and the last finishing in February. Students then select their thesis laboratory.

> [source line 209] Your Personal Statement should be no longer than two single-spaced pages. Your list of research publications is excluded from this two-page limit. In your list, please include the references, and at most, the associated abstracts. Please do not submit an entire manuscript unless it is unpublished. If there is a lapse in time for your education or employment, please explain why.

> [source line 210] At the end of the Personal Statement, list any research publications for which you are an author. The best personal statements show enthusiasm for science, your research experiences, and career goals. Convey to the committee that you are seriously considering the University of Minnesota based on your indication of specific labs who are doing work you would be interested in pursuing.

> [source line 222] You can either leave it blank or if you decide to add a description of your research, please include how you became interested in that area of research. Concisely describe what you were studying. Be sure to include which aspects of the research projects you extensively planned and developed. Describe your contributions to the implementation of the project. Indicate which direction you might take in the future if you continued in this line of research.

> [source line 223] Descriptions of Research should have minimal overlap with content in your Personal Statement and should be viewed as a complement and/or supplement to what is written in your Personal Statement.

> [source line 228] Y es, a CV or résumé is required.

> [source line 234] No. Unofficial transcripts should be uploaded to the application. Official transcripts are only required when you accept an offer of admission to the program. If you are an international student, go to the international student resources page for more information.

> [source line 238] We require applicants to submit three letters of recommendation. To ensure your application is as competitive as possible, carefully select recommenders based on the following guidelines:

> [source line 239] Primary Recommenders (Strongest Impact): We strongly encourage submitting multiple recommendations from faculty, principal investigators, or lab supervisors who have directly mentored or observed you in a research setting. These letters provide critical evidence of your aptitude for research, independent thought, resilience, and problem-solving abilities.

> [source line 265] No. The GRE general and subject scores are NOT required to apply to the program. Note that the University's Graduate School application system still includes a spot for GRE scores; however, you are not required to enter them. Please be aware that our application review committee will NOT view these scores, even if you do submit them.

Applications for each program are evaluated separately by different admissions committees. If you do apply to both, please indicate that in your personal statement. ### https://cbs.umn.edu/bmbb/graduate-program/prospective-students

> [source line 53] All students admitted to BMBB begin their first year rotating through three different labs in both BMBB and Molecular, Cellular, Developmental Biology and Genetics (MCDB&G). This enables each incoming graduate student to explore labs in both programs, decipher individual strengths and interests, and select the lab that best suits their career goals.

> [source line 54] During this first year, faculty from both programs offer advanced coursework in genetics, biochemistry, and molecular, cell, and developmental biology. Rotations last seven weeks, allowing students to acquaint themselves with the phenomenal facilities, technologies, and intellects on campus. This is how they begin the creative work of kindling ideas for their own novel research.
