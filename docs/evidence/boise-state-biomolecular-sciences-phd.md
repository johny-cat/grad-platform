Evidence anchors and exact source locations:
- Program heading: “The Biomolecular Sciences Ph.D. Program”. Official identity, interdisciplinary biomolecular research.
- FAQ: “minimum GPA of 3.00”; “an appropriate scientific discipline”; “a baccalaureate or graduate degree”. Funding section: all PhD applicants automatically considered; TA awards competitive, tuition/fee waiver, health insurance, annual $25, 000 (no year), third year moves to RA; after three program-funded years advisor grants cover assistantship. Interview/decision sections give mid-February/mid-March typical timings.
- Application materials: “Request three letters of recommendation”; “no more than 1750 words”; “names of 3-6 faculty members”; “OPTIONAL: Submit official Graduate Record Exam”. Bullets require scientific writing sample and CV/resume; FAQ requires transcripts.
- Deadlines: “January 10”; “May 7”; context explicitly “Fall 2026 admission”. Priority financial assistance in January, late assistance subject to availability; late applicants told to contact program.
- Waiver: “Domestic applicant (citizen or lawful permanent resident of the United States)”; “Overall GPA > 3.50”; “Prior research experience”; prerequisites required. Limited number, PhD only; subject line FA 25.
- International central: “$95 application fee (non-refundable)”; “unable to waive application fees for international graduate applicants”. Process overview lists official postsecondary transcripts/degree proof, English proof and passport. English table: IELTS 6.5, TOEFL 80, Duolingo 115; new TOEFL minimum being finalized. Educational exemption requires accredited/recognized degree from approved country AND English primary instruction; case-by-case additional proof possible. Test scores valid on receipt, directly from provider. Documents require certified literal translation if not English; evaluation performed internally.
- Rotations: “All students accepted into the program on teaching assistantships will complete three 5-week long rotations”; research assistantship students not required to rotate. Thus a single uniform program/lab admission model is omitted and routes explained.
