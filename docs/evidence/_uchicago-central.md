# University of Chicago central admissions evidence

Checked by opening the official pages on 2026-10-22 for Fall 2027 entry. Quotes below are factual source extracts, including headings and list items where the source does not use a full sentence. Each Chicago program evidence file identifies the applicable scope. No same-university program data was treated as evidence.

## UChicagoGRAD: English proficiency (BPHYS, GGSB, Neurobiology and PME)

Source: https://grad.uchicago.edu/admissions/apply/english-language-requirements/

- Requirement and accepted tests: "Applicants to the graduate schools and divisions of the University of Chicago, regardless of citizenship, must either meet one of our waiver criteria (below) or submit proof of English language proficiency." "Only the TOEFL iBT or IELTS Academic tests are accepted as proof of proficiency."
- Primary-language waiver: "If English has been a primary language of communication and schooling for you since childhood, you may attest to this on the application."
- Education waiver: "If you were enrolled for at least one full academic year in the last ten years, as a full-time, degree-seeking student at an accredited English-medium post-secondary institution in one of the following countries or territories:" The page then lists qualifying locations. The JSON refers to the University education waiver without reproducing that list.
- Validity: "TOEFL or IELTS score reports are valid for two years and scores will be considered expired if the test was taken more than two years prior to the application deadline."

Source: https://grad.uchicago.edu/admissions/apply/english-language-requirements/required-scores/

- The numerical table is explicitly labelled "*For the remainder of the 2026 admission cycle". Therefore its PSD and PME minimum scores are omitted from the Fall 2027 records. Current Biosciences FAQ scores are independently used below for its own programs.
- The omitted PSD row is headed "- All Other PSD Programs"; the omitted PME row is headed "Pritzker School of Molecular Engineering". Neither row is extrapolated to 2027.

## UChicagoGRAD: application scope and international requirements

Source: https://grad.uchicago.edu/admissions/apply/guidelines-and-deadlines/

- Separate applications: "Because the actual admissions process takes place within each school, division, and department, if you wish to apply to more than one program, you will need to create a separate application for each one, often in different online systems."
- International applicants: "There are no separate application requirements specifically for international students." "All applicants must meet our English proficiency requirement or qualify for a waiver, regardless of citizenship."
- Fee omission for BPHYS: "The applications to our programs require an accompanying fee." "This fee will be clearly indicated in the instructions on your application, and you will be prompted to pay the non-refundable application fee once you submit your application." No BPHYS or PSD amount is given on this page.
- Waiver process: "The fee waiver request form will be posted on your portal after the application is submitted."

Source: https://grad.uchicago.edu/admissions/apply/fee-waivers/

- Scope limitation: "Most graduate programs at the University of Chicago offer some application fee waivers, but the criteria vary by program." "There are six general types of fee waivers, but not all programs offer all of these waivers."
- The page does not explicitly state international eligibility for BPHYS. No general category is treated as proof that an international BPHYS applicant can obtain a waiver.

## UChicago Biosciences: current cycle and core requirements (GGSB and Neurobiology only)

Source: https://biosciences.uchicago.edu/admissions/how-to-apply

- Cycle and deadline: "The application period for UChicago Biosciences programs starting in Autumn Quarter 2027 will open in early September 2026." "Applicants are responsible for ensuring that all materials are submitted by the December 1, 2026 deadline."
- Application link: "Official online application" links to https://apply-bsd.uchicago.edu/. The JSON uses this opened instructions page as applyUrl.
- Formal GRE submission policy: "GRE test scores are optional." "The majority of our graduate programs have elected not to use submitted GRE scores as part of the review process." "The PhD programs in Public Health Sciences, Medical Physics, Biochemistry, and Molecular Biophysics do consider submitted GRE scores."
- Required transcripts: "All postsecondary education transcripts from which a degree was awarded". "Transcripts must be in the original language (with an authorized translation if that language is not English)." "Unofficial transcripts obtained from the school’s registrar are acceptable for the application review, but an official transcript displaying the seal of the degree-granting institution will be necessary before matriculation for admitted students."
- Statement: "Candidate Statement". "It should be clear, concise, well-crafted, and maximum of 2 pages."
- Letters: "Three letters of recommendation".
- Fee: "A non-refundable application fee of $90 is required."

Source: https://biosciences.uchicago.edu/admissions/how-to-apply/faq

- All materials and letters due: "For Autumn Quarter admission, the application deadline for all materials, including supplementary and letters of recommendation, is December 1, 2026 for all programs."
- Transcript seal requirement: "We require an unofficial transcript that displays the seal of the degree granting institution." This is retained in the GGSB and Neurobiology transcript document entries following independent second review.
- GRE nuance: "No, the GRE test is optional for all PHD programs." "The majority of UChicago Biosciences programs do not take GRE scores into consideration, and will not use them as part of the comprehensive review process even if they are supplied." "However, the programs in Medical Physics and Biochemistry and Molecular Biophysics will take GRE scores into consideration if available."
- Classification: the FAQ does not literally say "only" those programs consider scores, and it does not name GGSB or Neurobiology in a no-consideration sentence. Thus both records retain the explicit formal optional-submission value and explain this ambiguity. No program-specific consideration rule is invented.
- TOEFL old scale: "The minimum score (pre-January 21,2026) for admission to the BSD is 100 overall with subscores of no less than 26 in Reading, Writing, and Listening, and no less than 22 in Speaking."
- TOEFL new scale: "The minimum score (on/post-January 21,2026) for admission to the BSD is no less than 5 on Reading and Writing, no less than 5.5 on Listening, and no less than 4.5 Speaking". No new-scale overall score is invented.
- IELTS: "The minimum score for admission to the BSD is 7 overall with subscores of 7 each." The preceding sentence identifies the Academic test rather than General Training.
- Score validity in the current cycle: "TOEFL and IELTS scores are valid for two years based on the December 1, 2026 application deadline." "Therefore, the test date must have occurred December 1, 2024 or later."
- International acceptance and funding: "International applicants are welcome." "Our financial package is available to all students."

## UChicago Biosciences: funding and fee waivers (GGSB and Neurobiology only)

Source: https://biosciences.uchicago.edu/admissions/financial-support

- Funding guarantee: "UChicago Biosciences offers academically qualified applicants full and equitable support." "Such assistance is guaranteed through the student’s first five years of study, conditional on satisfactory degree progress, and is extended to completion of degree in all but the most unusual of circumstances."
- Coverage: "Financial support includes tuition and fees, and provides a stipend." "UChicago Biosciences provides supplemental funding to ensure that all students, regardless of funding source, receive the same standard of funding."
- Health insurance and old-year amount: "The 2026-27 stipend will be $47,741 plus health insurance and fees." The amount and stipend object are omitted because this is not 2027–28. Independent second review also removed health insurance from the Fall 2027 funding notes: this program-specific benefits sentence expressly names 2026–27, while the unqualified insurance sentence on the page speaks of "most PhD students" university-wide. The current Biosciences guarantee explicitly covers tuition, fees and a stipend; those remain stated.
- Waivers: "Generally, an application fee of $90 is required and non-refundable." "Applicants may qualify for specific fee waivers." "In order to request an application fee waiver, you must first submit an application."
- Portal and documentation: "Once an application is submitted, your application status portal will display the option to request a fee waiver by filling out a short form and providing documentation, if required."
- Payment timing: "If you intend to submit a fee waiver request, do not pay the application fee until you receive a decision about your request." "If you pay the application fee, and later have your waiver request approved, the fee will not be refunded."
- Categories are headed "University of Chicago degree program students and alumni", "Current or recent participants in qualifying programs", "Current undergraduates receiving need-based aid from a U.S. institution", "Valid fee waiver codes received from an authorized representative of the University of Chicago", and "Other waiver categories".
- International fee-waiver eligibility is not explicitly stated. Both records use waiver=unknown; general waiver availability is not represented as international availability.
